One disability that I have (some) experience with is limited vision/blindness. While this can be (at best) very frustrating, it has also led to some major advancements and benefits to humanity as a whole. The fact that people can have limited to no eyesight means that visual mediums are not necessarily sufficient to convey a message or an idea. This has led to the rise of audio description and non-visual media. This not only allows for media to be accessible to those who are visually impaired, but also challenges us to think about different ways to convey various forms of media in a more critical manner. 

This has resulted in further technological advancements including OCR (optical character recognition) which can recognize written and printed text and process it for use in other widely used technologies such as audiobooks, voice assistants and document scanners. Additionally, the development of text to speech and audio visual description has been impactful in not just enabling vision impaired people to also consume visual media, but also for people without vision impairments to operate relatively hands-free in various situations. 

Beyond just technology, the need to accommodate visual-impaired people has led to safer design of public spaces and roads. Audible pedestrian signals and tactile crosswalks makes it easy for visually impaired people to navigate the outside world, as well as ensure that distracted drivers are aware of pedestrians and do not cause injuries while driving.
