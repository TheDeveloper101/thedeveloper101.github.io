For this blog post, we are expected to analyze a particular painting. The painting I chose was the portrait of Susan Nussbaum by Riva Lehrer as a part of her _Circle Stories_ series of paintings. The portrait is very striking, and a number of details immediately stand out. We see that Susan herself is predominantly featured, but off to the side and in a wheelchair. For important context, Susan Nussbaum was struck by a car at the age of 24, causing her to lose mobility in her legs and only partial usage of her arms. Immediately behind her though, we see trees and grass, along with roads stretching across. To me, the roads seem almost similar to a stream. In some sense, the mobility that waterways used to provide back in the day is now the function that roads serve as, and the way people would swim/boat along rivers, creeks and streams is the same way that people now drive, walk, or take public transit along highways, roads, and alleyways. 

Moving to the foreground, there are a couple things that stand out quite clearly. The first is that Susan is facing the viewer, and her body has a deliberate gesture to it. Because of the way she is turned along with the placement of her hands, it appears she is taking on an active role. Her pose is almost reminiscent of a faculty member speaking to a student during office hours. Additionally, her placement of her right hand over her waist seems deliberate, almost as if it is delineating the separation between what she does and does not have control over. Behind Susan, we see a black railing. This suggests that we are looking at Susan as she is on a balcony, which provides many rich interpretations. In particular, the balcony serves as a form of separation. This separation occurs in two different ways. First, the railing shows that Susan is separated from the environment. This is both in terms of her lack of mobility due to being in a wheelchair, along with the fact that the railing physically prevents her from moving off the balcony. Secondly, we see a gray rod, which suggests that we may be looking at her through a screen door. This serves as a way to not just separate Susan from the audience, but also serves as an illustration for the social stigma of being disabled. We can see and observe the disabled person, but like looking through a screen door, they are separated and left out of "the rest of society" - which in this case refers to the rest of the house/apartment this balcony is a part of. 

This separation also relates in particular to how disabled people are able to interact with the environment. Just like gazing from a balcony, many disabled people are only ever able to view nature from afar, but never actually experience it personally. This is due to not just a result of nature itself being unaccommodating, but also structural obstacles that limit the accessibility of places that have otherwise been modified. Kafer discusses this when she says "They ignore the complicated histories of who is granted permission to enter nature, where nature is said to reside and how one must move." While nature of course is not always accommodating even to those who are able-bodied, the lack of accessibility for disabled people is quickly met with "well we cannot modify nature," ignoring the fact that nature has already in many cases been modified to allow for us to experience it. 

Coming back to the painting, to the right of Susan, we see a number of objects which also have further symbolic meaning. Obviously, the steering wheel on fire refers to her accident, serving as a reminder that anyone, no matter their health can become disabled at any time. As frequently mentioned on the internet, being disabled is the one minority you can join at any time. Additionally, we see some other objects which refer to particular aspects of Susan's life and personality. The makeup-related object (unfortuantely I do not know its name) show's Susan smiling in it's reflection, showing how she is still able to obtain joy no matter the circumstances. The pencil and paper refer to not just Susan's occupation as a playwright, but also to her activism on behalf of disabled people. Susan wrote a very well known book _Good Kings, Bad Kings_ which discusses what it means to be disabled and the experiences that disabled people have. 

This painting has a lot of very deep meaning, not just in terms of highlighting disability, but also considering what it means for someone to be disabled and to interact with the environment and nature more broadly. The way Susan Nussbaum is portrayed really highlights the cruel irony that many disabled people face - they are able to see able bodied individuals exist in and interact with the world with ease, while they are separated away (metaphorically and physically so!) and forced to observe from a distance.
